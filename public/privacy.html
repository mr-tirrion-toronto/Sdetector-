DATA & PRIVACY

Privacy Policy

Effective date: 27 November 2026 · Version 1.6

Privacy principle: Submit only public URLs you are authorised to investigate. Do not submit private links, credentials, personal records or payment information. A submitted URL may be stored as part of a scan record and limited data may be sent to external analysis providers.

1. Information we process

  • Scan information: submitted and normalised URL, hostname, scan time, risk score, risk level and generated findings.
  • Technical results: DNS, TLS, certificate-transparency, domain-registration, threat-feed and public-page inspection results when available.
  • Account information: information needed to create and operate an account, authenticate sessions and manage API keys. API keys are stored as hashes; the raw key is shown only when created.
  • Operational information: limited request and rate-limit metadata needed to protect the service against abuse and maintain reliability.

1A. Message, phone and attachment checks

When you use the multi-channel analyzer, the submitted message text is processed to produce local rule-based findings. Raw SMS, email bodies, WhatsApp/Telegram text, social messages, phone numbers and decoded QR payloads are not stored in the Scam Detector database. For signed-in users, the service may store the channel, risk score, risk level, generated findings and timestamp to provide account history. Guest analyses are not added to account history. QR images are decoded in the browser where supported and are not uploaded. Attachment inspection reads file metadata, selected header bytes and a SHA-256 hash locally; the file itself is not uploaded, executed or sandboxed.

Phone-number checks use the number submitted for analysis. If an optional phone-intelligence provider is configured, the number is sent to that provider to return validity, carrier, country, line type and available risk data. A carrier or VoIP result does not identify a subscriber or prove SIM registration ownership.

2. Why information is used

We process information to run scans, generate reports, provide account and API functionality, maintain scan history, detect abuse, troubleshoot errors, improve detection quality and maintain the security of the service.

3. External providers and data sharing

Some scan checks rely on infrastructure or data providers, which may include Cloudflare services, Neon database hosting, Firecrawl page extraction, PhishDestroy domain intelligence, public DNS resolvers, RDAP services, certificate-transparency data, Phishunt, Abstract Phone Validation (only when configured for phone lookups), and (only when configured) Google Web Risk, URLhaus, PhishTank and VirusTotal Premium. These providers process the limited information needed for their function under their own terms and privacy practices.

The PhishDestroy domain-intelligence lookup receives the normalised hostname rather than the full submitted URL. Page-content inspection uses a sanitised public URL and is subject to public IP validation and size/time limits. Optional external reputation lookups send the normalised scheme, hostname and path to the configured provider APIs; URL usernames/passwords, query strings and fragments are stripped first. A secret embedded inside a URL path may still be transmitted, so do not submit password-reset, private invitation or other sensitive URLs. External providers may retain or further process submitted indicators under their own terms. Google Safe Browsing APIs are non-commercial only; commercial checks use Google Web Risk. VirusTotal Public API is not used for commercial service; a separately licensed Premium API key is required. URLhaus Community API commercial eligibility must be confirmed with URLhaus.

The Independent Evidence Workbench also creates optional links to external company registries, search engines, public threat-history services and consumer-protection guidance. These links are not opened automatically. When you choose to open one, your browser sends the domain name and any public page-title or operator-name search terms embedded in the link to that third-party service. The third party then processes the request under its own privacy policy. Scam Detector does not receive the external search results automatically or control how those sites use data.

4. Storage and retention

Scan records and findings are stored in the service database so scans can be displayed, reported and associated with accounts where applicable. Account credentials are protected using password hashing and session controls; API keys are stored as hashes. Threat-feed indicators and synchronization status are stored to support threat matching and feed-health reporting.

Evidence vault: When a signed-in user opens a report for a scan owned by that account, Scam Detector may create a server-side evidence manifest containing the persisted scan metadata, stored findings and retained intelligence snapshot/assessments. The manifest includes a SHA-256 digest. The service may also store hash-linked application events recording manifest creation, integrity verification and initiation of the browser print/PDF dialog. The service cannot confirm that a PDF was actually saved. After saving a PDF, a user may optionally select that local PDF in the report page; the browser computes its SHA-256 digest locally and sends only the digest, file size and event metadata to the service. The PDF bytes are not uploaded or stored by this feature. When Ed25519 signing is configured, the evidence record also stores the digital signature, public key and key identifier; the private signing key is held as a Cloudflare secret and is not stored in the database. The digest can later be compared with a newly selected copy. Guest scans and scans not owned by the signed-in account are not added to that account evidence vault. The manifest does not contain raw HTTP traffic, every transient third-party response, original website files or the PDF bytes.

Evidence records and custody events are account-linked and are designed to be append-only through the application, but they are not immutable against privileged database access, independently trusted timestamps or digital signatures. Keep independent copies with a trusted custodian when evidence preservation is important.

Retention periods, deletion workflows and backup lifetimes may depend on operational and provider constraints. The service does not currently promise a fixed deletion schedule or automatic deletion of every copy in backups. Do not use Scam Detector to store confidential information.

5. Security

We use security controls appropriate to the service architecture, including server-side secrets, access controls for private account features, rate limiting, password hashing and safeguards around external URL inspection. No internet service or storage system can be guaranteed completely secure. Notify us through the repository's responsible disclosure process if you discover a vulnerability.

6. Your choices and requests

Do not provide unnecessary personal information. Keep account credentials and API keys private, and revoke exposed API keys. For account or privacy questions, use the contact channel published in the official repository. Requests will be reviewed subject to identity verification, applicable law, security needs and technical retention constraints.

7. Children

Scam Detector is a general-purpose security-awareness tool and is not designed to collect information from children. Do not create an account or submit personal information belonging to a child.

8. International processing

Our infrastructure and service providers may process information in countries other than your own. By using the service, you understand that cross-border processing may occur, subject to applicable law and provider safeguards.

9. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as features, providers and legal requirements change. The effective date and version at the top will identify the current published version.
